# RBI Policy Timeline Sample for RAG Evaluation

> Sample document for RAG testing. This is a simplified RBI-style policy dataset created for document question-answering evaluation. It is not an official RBI circular.

## Document Purpose

This document contains a year-wise sample timeline of Reserve Bank of India monetary policy actions, internal policy IDs, repo rate movements, stance updates, liquidity notes, and transmission observations. It is designed to test whether a RAG system can answer questions using exact years, policy IDs, rate values, and multiple pieces of evidence.

## Key Terms

- Repo rate: The policy rate at which the Reserve Bank of India lends short-term funds to banks.
- Reverse repo rate: The rate at which banks park funds with the Reserve Bank of India.
- CRR: Cash Reserve Ratio, the share of bank deposits kept with the Reserve Bank of India.
- SLR: Statutory Liquidity Ratio, the share of deposits maintained in approved liquid assets.
- MCLR: Marginal Cost of Funds based Lending Rate.
- WALR: Weighted Average Lending Rate.
- WADTDR: Weighted Average Domestic Term Deposit Rate.
- Accommodation withdrawal: A stance indicating the central bank wants to gradually reduce excess liquidity support.
- Neutral stance: A stance where the central bank is not pre-committed to tightening or easing.

## Policy ID Reference Notes

- Policy IDs beginning with `RBI-MPC` refer to sample Monetary Policy Committee decisions.
- Policy IDs beginning with `RBI-MPR` refer to sample Monetary Policy Report updates.
- The suffix format is `YEAR-MONTH-SEQUENCE`, for example `RBI-MPC-2025-DEC-06`.
- The 2026 February update is an assessment note, not a rate-setting meeting.

## Transmission Summary

During tightening periods such as 2022 and early 2023, external benchmark-linked loans reflected repo rate increases faster than older MCLR-linked loans. Deposit rates also increased, but usually with a lag because banks adjusted funding costs gradually.

During the 2025 easing cycle, the repo rate declined from 6.50% before February 2025 to 5.25% by December 2025, a cumulative reduction of 125 bps. Transmission to outstanding loans was clearer than to fresh loans in the February 2026 update. The median 1-year MCLR declined by 20 bps in H2:2025-26. Fresh rupee loan WALR increased by 5 bps because loan volumes shifted toward sectors with higher interest rates, while outstanding loan WALR declined by 26 bps.

On deposits, outstanding domestic term deposit rates declined by 20 bps in H2:2025-26 up to February 2026. Fresh domestic term deposit rates increased by 4 bps, showing that banks still competed for new deposits even during the easing cycle.

## Liquidity and Credit Conditions

In 2020 and 2021, liquidity support was used to maintain credit flow during the pandemic recovery period. In 2022 and 2023, policy focused more on inflation control and withdrawal of accommodation. In 2024, the policy rate remained unchanged while the stance eventually shifted to neutral. In 2025, easing resumed, but lending and deposit rate transmission varied across loan type, deposit type, and bank funding conditions.
